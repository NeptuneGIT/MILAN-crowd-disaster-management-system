    How the dots map to the maths
    1. Real scale. The patch is a real piece of the mela (default 80 m × 50 m). Dots = density × area, so the Bluetooth range circle is drawn at true size. A mela-wide crowd would be about 17,000 patches like this one. The crowd wraps around the edges.
    2. Phones. Each person is an active app phone with probability smartphone × Android × installed × scanning. Wristband wearers are not phones (the family is elsewhere). The share of people wearing bands does not change any band's chance of being located.
    3. Movement. Every 10 s round, each person steps in a slowly turning direction. The step size is walking speed (Weidmann speed-density curve: 1.34 m/s free flow, near zero at about 5.4 people/m²) × the "crowd turnover" control. The turnover control stands for relative motion: neighbours in a flowing crowd move together, so only part of walking speed brings new phones into range.
    4. Hearing. Median range is the Bluetooth range control, the same at every density (no link-budget estimate). Each band–phone pair also has its own signal variation (6 dB, drawn from a bell curve and held for 30 s), so some phones hear a band from farther than the median and some do not hear it from closer.
    5. Reporting. Each round, a phone that can hear a band catches it with the catch rate, needs a GPS fix, and uploads with the upload-success probability (which halves at 6 people/m²). The first successful upload is a "located" band.
    6. The defaults are deliberately pessimistic: each is set to the low end of its plausible range (smartphones 40%, scanning 40%, range 4 m, catch rate 40%, turnover 0.15, GPS 70%, upload success 60%). Only the installed share was tuned, until 99% of bands were located within 90 minutes at camp density. Change any control to see how much margin there is.
    7. Located means the server received a position for a flagged band. It excludes the time to flag the dependent, reach them and reunite them. Every band is treated as if flagged missing at time zero.
    8. Closed form. The "maths predicts" figure uses the same chain without simulating individuals; the two agree within about 5 percentage points; the closed form runs a little optimistic under harsh settings like these defaults (about 4 points early on).
